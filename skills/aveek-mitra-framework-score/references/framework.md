# Framework source map

Principles distilled from Aveek Mitra's interview (ValuePickr, "Conversation with Kushal", Oct 2026) and
how each became a scoring rule. Read this when the user asks *why* a rule exists or wants to tune weights.

## 1. Growth is non-negotiable → Pillar A (25 pts) + growth gate
- "Unless the company is growing why should I invest." Vesuvius India (well-managed refractories, growth
  tied to the blast-furnace steel cycle) and Ambika Cotton (conservative promoter who won't grow) are
  "good companies, not good investments".
- Rule: A1–A4 score sales/PAT CAGR, consistency and momentum. **Gate:** sales and PAT 3y CAGR both < 8% → cap 55
  and label "Good company, not a good investment".

## 2. Reinvest at high ROE beats paying dividends → Pillar B (20 pts)
- Prefers a firm redeploying FCF at ROE ≥ its historical ROE over a 5% dividend yield (ITC example).
- Cash hoarded on the books earns only treasury yield and drags ROCE ("a cash pile is flat for me").
- Rules: B1 ROCE, B2 ROE, B3 incremental ROCE vs average ROCE, B4 idle-cash share of assets, B5 CFO/PAT.

## 3. Moat vs competitive advantage; niche global leaders → Pillar C (20 pts)
- Shivalik Bimetal: "collective wisdom generated by manufacturing refined materials over time is itself the moat";
  a tiny but critical part needed in huge numbers (EVs, switchgear, smart meters); can become a global leader.
- Force Motors: superior design (monocoque Urbania) → segment dominance.
- Prefers global long cycles/trends (electrification, AI, social-media advertising) over local cycles.
- Rules: C1 margin stability, C2 OPM vs peers, C3 niche leadership, C4 critical/low cost share, C5 know-how barrier,
  C6 structural tailwind, C7 business type.

## 4. Local cyclicals are hard; picks-and-shovels are OK → C7 + cap
- Sugar, cement, regional steel: prices set by micro-market or government, hard to predict (KCP example).
- Acceptable: pick-and-shovel plays on domestic steel demand (seamless pipes, structurals) and deleveraging stories
  where quarter-on-quarter balance-sheet improvement is visible (Maharashtra Seamless).
- Rule: `local_commodity_cyclical` → cap 55 unless reclassified as `picks_and_shovels`.

## 5. Promoter behaviour and governance → Pillar D (15 pts) + kill switches
- Shivalik: promoter bought a relative's stake at ~Rs 610, near market price — gave comfort to re-enter.
- TRIL: promoter "hunger" to win market. Kitex: unexplained ~Rs 200 cr held in foreign banks → sold.
- Railway names: accounting concerns, receivable and FCF problems → must be discounted probabilistically.
- Rules: D1 promoter holding change, D2 promoter buy price vs CMP, D3 pledge, D4 receivable-days trend, D5 hunger.
  Kill switches: auditor issues (cap 30), unexplained cash / related party (cap 40), accounting concerns with CFO/PAT < 0.5 (cap 40),
  pledge > 50% (cap 30).

## 6. Buy the inflection, not the certainty → Pillar E (10 pts)
- "If everything becomes certain, a stock becomes a bond." Returns come from investing through uncertainty.
- TRIL bought loss-making on a discom-recovery thesis; Force Motors tracked monthly production / capacity;
  Hemisphere Properties = deep value with no trigger → value trap.
- Rules: E1 loss→profit, E2 deleveraging, E3 volume/utilisation data, E4 new-product adoption, E5 dated unlock trigger.
  Asset play without E5 evidence → cap 50, "value trap risk".

## 7. P/E is perception of growth × durability → Pillar F (10 pts) + implied-growth diagnostic
- "P/E is nothing but a perception of earnings… a proxy of future cash flows and growth rate."
  You cannot justify very high P/Es (aerospace names) without a view on growth *durability* (terminal value).
- TAM trap: "people take total addressable market which is much bigger than what actually is addressable."
- Rules: F1 relative PEG vs peers, F2 P/E vs own 5y median, F3 **addressable** market headroom.
  Diagnostic: EPS CAGR implied by current P/E (hurdle 13%, exit P/E 20, 10y by default).

## 8. Sell discipline; portfolio return over multibaggers → sell module
- Sells when growth flattens, P/E exceeds competitors without superior growth, segment TAM is limited (Urbania can't
  replace every bus), or a better alternative exists. Doesn't try to sell at the peak; happily re-buys lower
  (Force Motors 1,340 → 10,000 → re-bought ~6,100 → sold 15–17k; Shivalik sold, re-bought).
- Signals S1–S5 → HOLD / REVIEW / TRIM / SELL CANDIDATE.

## 8b. v2 additions (from validation against his actual decisions - see validation.md)
- **Sell the run, re-buy the consolidation** (Shivalik: sold after 5-7x, re-entered lower after a promoter bought at
  ~Rs 610; Force Motors: sold ~10,000, re-bought ~6,100) → "quality in consolidation" detector; growth gate waived.
- **Invest through uncertainty in turnarounds** (TRIL loss-making 2016; Force Motors after FY21-22 losses) → automatic
  turnaround mode: inflection 20 pts, price/sales and normalised P/E instead of PEG.
- **Growth the industry can't give** (Kitex: fewer kids per woman, price-point ceiling) → S6 implied growth vs industry growth.
- **Growth tied to a capped end-market** (Vesuvius: refractories as a proxy for blast-furnace steel) → evidence-gated
  `structural_headwind` cap at 60.

## 9. Sizing and verification
- Starts ~2–4% depending on conviction; cash ~5%; probabilistic assessment then allocation decision.
- Scuttlebutt: plant/AGM visits, shipment-tracking data, customer checks (tour operators), ex-employees/engineers.
- Implementation: sizing bands from the score; evidence confidence from distinct scuttlebutt types; Low confidence caps starter at 2%.
