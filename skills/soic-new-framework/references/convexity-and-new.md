# Convexity and the NEW taxonomy

The video's first principle is to look for **convex** outcomes: a business whose
profits can grow non-linearly, rather than a capped 10–15% compounder.
`[00:03:25]`–`[00:05:23]`

A concave activity has a known, bounded payoff. A fixed deposit is the simplest
example. A portfolio of expensive 10–15% growers is also concave because the
return can be eaten by P/E de-rating. A convex activity has an asymmetric payoff:
the downside is limited relative to the possible upside.

This is why the screen is not a “cheap stock” screen. The question is whether the
earnings band can change.

## NEW checklist

The video lists these forms of “new” `[00:10:51]`:

- new market leaders
- new supply-demand setups
- new institutional owners
- new products
- new management
- new highs
- new earnings run-rates
- new market trends

They are not interchangeable. A new product is not a new manager. A new
institutional owner is not proof of a new earnings run-rate. Record which one is
actually present.

## Theme expiry

“Every theme comes with an expiry date.” `[00:07:47]` A new policy or new demand
can create a J-curve, but new supply can later compress margins even while demand
grows. The solar sector is the video's example: ALMM created the opportunity, then
capacity additions changed the supply-demand balance.

Do not treat a theme as permanently supportive. Record its stage and expiry risk.
