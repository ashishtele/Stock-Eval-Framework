# Theme expiry and the capital cycle

A theme is a tailwind, not a permanent law. The video warns that every theme has
an expiry date `[00:07:47]` and gives solar as the example:

1. A new policy (ALMM/ALCM) created domestic demand.
2. Capacity was scarce, so margins and earnings expanded.
3. New supply arrived; module prices fell and margins compressed.

The same logic applies to the paints and cables examples `[00:43:13]`–`[00:44:29]`:
if 30–35% of an industry's capital employed is injected at once, industry ROCE
can fall even if absolute EBIT does not. A company can grow and still de-rate.

## Context fields

When adding a theme to the memo, record:

- theme name
- why the company is exposed
- stage: emerging / crowded / oversupplied / declining
- expiry risk: low / medium / high
- evidence and date
- the specific supply response you are watching

Do not put a theme score into the J-curve. It is a separate risk.

## Warning signs

- capacity announcements from several competitors
- imports becoming cheaper
- policy support being withdrawn
- price erosion in tenders
- industry capex growing faster than demand
- the company's ROCE falling while sales grow

The screen can show the company's ROCE and CFO, but it cannot see the industry
capital cycle. That requires a separate peer and capacity dataset.
